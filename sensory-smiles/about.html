Behind our mission

Every child deserves moments of peace.

Yet for 1 in 6 children facing sensory challenges, calm can feel out of reach. Sensory tools are often expensive or inaccessible—leaving neurodiverse individuals and those experiencing anxiety without the support they need.

Sensory Smiles changes that.

Founded in Hangzhou in 2025 by high school student Shi Qi Ooi, we are an international nonprofit designing accessible sensory toys that reduce anxiety, manage overload, and promote emotional regulation.

The Story Behind the Smile

Shi Qi's inspiration came from her own collection of fidget toys and stuffed animals—tools she relied on for stress relief. “What if I could combine both,” she wondered, “and make the ultimate comforting toy—accessible to everyone?”

The answer became clear in December 2025. While volunteering at a Love21 Foundation event in Hong Kong, Shi Qi watched children with autism and Down syndrome light up as they squeezed squishy sensory toys during a magic show. Their joy was immediate, unfiltered, and revealing. Sensory tools weren't just nice to have—they were essential.

That moment crystallized her vision: design toys that soothe, then get them into the hands who need them most.

Our Impact: Just Beginning

After coordinating with manufacturers for prototype production, we distributed our first 200 toys across 6 service centers in Hangzhou. By year's end, we aim to place 1,000+ toys in the hands of individuals who need them—across Hangzhou, Hong Kong, and Massachusetts.

We're building something bigger than toys. We're building a community that believes sensory support should be a right, not a privilege.

Powered by Community

Sensory Smiles has already raised over $20,000 from our school community. From this, we're honored to receive over $18,000 from the CIS Student Dream Fund in AY 2025-26, covering prototype development and manufacturing for our first donation batches.

Our gratitude runs deep. Every dollar, every share, every “have you heard about Sensory Smiles?” moves us closer to a world where no one faces sensory distress alone.

Why sensory tools?

Our Evidence

Calming sensory tools work best when inputs are gentle, predictable, and customizable.

200+

Users reached across community partners

What designers learn from research

Soft, predictable sensory input—touch, gentle pressure, mild scent, and simple fidget action—can help people feel safer and more regulated in overwhelming environments.

Pastel colours

Softer colours, less visual noise

Muted colours and clear forms can lower visual load and support calmer engagement for people with sensory differences.

De Silva, 2023; Norouzi et al., 2024

Soft texture

Pleasant touch that soothes

Soft, fur-like textures are often preferred in comfort objects and can help filter out harsher sensory input.

Macdonald et al., 2024; MacLennan et al., 2021

Squishy fill

Hug, squeeze, ground

Squishable, compressible materials offer proprioceptive feedback linked to grounding, safety, and reduced physical tension.

Park et al., 2020; Sahoo et al., 2025

Lavender scent

A mild calm cue

Gentle lavender scent is associated with relaxation and emotional comfort—though scent should stay optional, since strong smells can overwhelm some people.

Zhu et al., 2026

Fidget element

Hands busy, mind steadier

Fidget tools can support repetitive hand action and may reduce anxiety most for sensory-seeking children.

Roche et al., 2023

The big idea

Gentle. Predictable. Customizable.

Across autism, ADHD, and broader neurodevelopmental groups, tactile and emotionally resonant inputs repeatedly help with regulation—but preferences vary, so choice matters.

Brandes-Aitken et al., 2024; Jurek et al., 2025; Bray et al., 2025

What partners told us

  • Organizations reported stress relief, better mood, calmer behaviour, and more engagement in activities.
  • A rehabilitation centre said the toys could shorten negative emotional episodes for some people with intellectual disabilities.

Important caveats

Everyone is different. No single feature works for every sensory profile.

Customization. Over- and under-responsive users often need different intensities.

Scent intensity. A balanced, mild scent can support calm; too strong or too faint may reduce comfort.

Fidget evidence varies. Benefits appear strongest for sensory seekers.

Our next step: keep the soft, calming design—and offer more choice in texture, appearance, portability, and optional scent.
